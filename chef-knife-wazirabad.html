Wazirabad steel, Sialkot craft — and the difference a forged blade makes.

Wazirabad sits 40 km south of Sialkot on the same GT Road industrial spine that has produced cutlery, surgical instruments and sporting goods for over a century. Both cities work in metal. But they have specialised differently, and the difference shows in the blade.

Wazirabad's cutlery industry produces utility knives sold by weight in bazaars — functional, affordable, produced at volume. These knives are stamped from thin stainless steel sheet, not forged from solid bar stock. The blade metal hardens less precisely under the stamp than under the hammer, holds an edge for weeks rather than months, and the handle is riveted on rather than formed as one continuous piece of metal with the tang.

Pakistan Knife Factory's chef knife is hand-forged: shaped by hammer and heat from a high-carbon stainless steel billet, ground by hand, mirror-polished by hand. The full-tang construction means the blade and handle are one uninterrupted piece of metal running the full length of the grip. The balance point is set deliberately; the weight of the blade does half the cutting work. Each knife takes longer to produce — and that is precisely why it outperforms everything stamped at volume.
