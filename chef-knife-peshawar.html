Peshawari food — built for serious knife work.

Peshawar produces some of the most demanding cooking in South Asia. The Peshawari kitchen is built on a few techniques executed with precision: high-heat lamb over open fire, confident spicing, fat rendered correctly, long-cook pulao on charcoal. Getting any of these right demands tools that match the ambition.

Chapli kebab — Peshawar's most exported dish — requires mincing beef or mutton with onion, tomato, coriander, green chilli, egg and spices to a specific coarse texture. The 8-inch chef knife, used as a chopper in short fast strokes on a heavy board, produces exactly that texture without over-processing the meat. A food processor turns it to paste. A blunt knife tears it. Only a sharp forged blade chops it right. The same knife minces the onion and ginger mixture fine, then combines them cleanly without releasing excess moisture — a small detail that makes the difference between a kebab that holds its shape and one that falls apart.

Namkeen karahi — Peshawar's other defining dish — demands whole bone-in mutton or goat separated at the joints, cooked fast at high heat with ginger and green chillies. The chef knife separates the pieces cleanly at the joint without splintering bone into the meat.

Kabuli pulao is built on caramelised onion: two or three large onions sliced thin and even. Uneven slices cook unevenly and produce bitter patches that ruin the pulao. The 8-inch blade held at a consistent angle produces uniform slices in seconds. Tabbakhana, Namak Mandi and Nishtar Hall set the standard. Households in Hayatabad, University Town and Cantt who cook at that level at home need equipment that matches. The PKF - Pakistan Knives Factory set was built for exactly that kitchen.
